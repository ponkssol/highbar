Most "pump.fun alternative" pages are written for people who want to launch a coin. Traders have a different checklist: the fee on the confirmation screen, whether an official-app surcharge applies, where the coin trades after it graduates, and what can be checked on-chain before a buy. This guide uses pump.fun's official [fees page](https://pump.fun/docs/fees), updated 16 November 2026, and the official fee pages for the other products named here. It is not a recommendation to buy or sell any token. Highbar is our product and is one option in the list, not the default winner.

## What traders actually pay on pump.fun

On the curve, official docs list **1.25%**, with 0.30% to the creator, 0.95% to the protocol and 0% to LP. After graduation, coins trade on PumpSwap, where official canonical fee tiers run from 1.250% down to 0.300%; this article does not flatten those tiers into one number. Official docs also list a 0.015 SOL graduation fee.

Separately, official pump.fun apps charge a **Platform Fee** of 0.45% or 0.40% from 17 October 2026. Official docs say that fee is not charged in PumpSwap or the Terminal. A trader who only remembers "1.25%" and then buys in an official app can be surprised. Read the confirmation screen. Our [pump.fun fees explainer](/articles/pump-fun-fees-explained) walks through the same official table.

This article does not state a pump.fun graduation-threshold market cap or SOL target, because the official fees and bonding-curve pages used here do not publish that number.

## How other official fee tables compare, for traders

These are published trader-facing fees, not a ranking:

| Venue | Official trader-facing fee, in brief | After the threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pump.fun curve | 1.25%, plus official-app Platform Fee of 0.45% or 0.40% | PumpSwap, canonical tiers 1.250% to 0.300% |
| Highbar | 1% on the curve and 1% in DAMM v2; no extra website fee; extension 0.02% | Locked Meteora DAMM v2 pool |
| Jupiter Studio | 1% or 2% for life, split with Jupiter; anti-sniper can start at 99% for 1–5 minutes | Current configs: permanently locked DAMM v2 |
| Raydium LaunchLab | Default 1% on the curve; usual 0.25% CPMM fee after graduation | Raydium CPMM path |
| Moonshot official app | Coin fee 0.5% pre-bond, 0.3% post-bond, plus app tiers of 2.5% or 1% by size | Bonds at a $1 million market cap |
| Heaven | 1% protocol fee below $100k mcap, then 0.25% or 0.5%, plus a creator fee | Stays on Heaven's AMM |

A 1% Highbar pool fee can be cheaper than a 2% Studio token or a 2.5% Moonshot app trade, and more expensive than a 0.25% LaunchLab CPMM trade or a low PumpSwap canonical tier. Those comparisons only hold if you are on the phase the row describes. An anti-sniper window — Highbar's optional 25% to about 1% over 60 seconds, or Studio's 99% to 1% or 2% over 1 to 5 minutes — can dominate the first minutes. Neither stops bots.

## What "alternative" means if you are trading, not launching

An alternative is useful only if it changes a rule you actually care about. If you wanted to avoid pump.fun's official-app Platform Fee, trading in PumpSwap or the Terminal is the official way to do that on pump.fun itself; moving to another launchpad is a different decision. If you wanted locked liquidity after graduation, Highbar and current Jupiter Studio configs document permanent LP locks; this article does not invent a PumpSwap lock rule. If you wanted a lower post-graduation fee, official LaunchLab CPMM and some PumpSwap tiers are lower than Highbar's 1% DAMM v2 fee.

Highbar remains a small, new venue. Its [docs](/docs) and [launch page](/launch) are for people who also want to launch there. Traders can use the [Highbar Chrome extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/oioebamfkpahdnjkbbmldepkackabhed) to trade from an X post, and should still confirm the mint, the pool and the 0.02% extension fee on the confirmation screen. No fee table in this article is a reason to buy a token.

Locked liquidity and a creator fee share are design choices. They do not make a token safe, and they do not stop sniper bots on their own.

Always confirm fee rates, graduation targets and liquidity rules on the live docs, or on the on-chain config, immediately before you sign.

A useful way to use a comparison like this is to write down the three rules you actually care about — the curve fee, the creator share, and what happens to liquidity at graduation — and check those three lines on each official page, rather than choosing a launchpad by name or by a social-media clip.

If you are launching, prepare the token name, ticker, image and any links first, then read the fee table for the exact config you are about to sign, including whether an anti-sniper or app fee applies. If you are trading, check the mint, the pool, and the live fee on the confirmation screen.

None of the platforms compared here is affiliated with Highbar, and a factual comparison is not a recommendation to use one of them, or to buy any token that has already launched.

Take your time, read the confirmation screen, and only sign if the live fee and destination pool match what you expected to pay.
