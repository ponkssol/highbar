If you trade Solana memecoins, you connect your wallet to a lot of things: launchpads, trading terminals, browser extensions, airdrop checkers and sites you found in a reply on X. Every one of those is a chance to sign something you should not. A **burner wallet** is the simplest way to cap the damage.

This guide explains what a burner wallet is, how to set one up, how to fund it and the habits that keep your main wallet out of reach. We run Highbar, a Solana launchpad and a Chrome extension for trading from X. The advice below applies to any platform.

## What a burner wallet is

A burner wallet is a separate wallet used only for risky activity. It holds a small balance, connects to new apps and does the fast trading. Your main wallet holds savings and long-term positions, and it rarely or never connects to anything new.

The idea is not that the burner is "safe". It is that it is **expendable**. If its key leaks, if you sign a malicious transaction, or if an extension misbehaves, the loss is limited to what was in the burner.

## Why memecoin traders need one

Memecoin trading combines several risks that are rare elsewhere:

- **New sites every day.** Launchpads, copycat front ends and "claim" pages appear constantly.
- **Speed over checking.** Trading from a timeline encourages one-click decisions.
- **Look-alike tokens.** Copycat tickers trick people into buying the wrong contract.
- **Phishing around news.** Platform changes are followed by fake "migration" pages. A current example is the wave of guidance around pump.fun ending external wallet sign-ins on 30 November 2026, reported by [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/pump-fun-ends-external-wallet-login/).
- **Browser-stored keys.** Extensions that store keys locally put them in your browser profile.

A burner wallet does not remove any of these. It shrinks what each one can cost you.

## How to set one up

### Step 1: Create a wallet with its own recovery phrase

Most Solana wallets let you add extra accounts under the same recovery phrase. That is convenient, but a leaked phrase then exposes every account under it. For a true burner, create a wallet with **its own** recovery phrase, either as a separate wallet profile or in a separate browser profile.

### Step 2: Back it up properly

Write the recovery phrase down offline. Do not save it in notes apps, screenshots or cloud drives. A burner is expendable, but losing access to it still loses what is inside.

### Step 3: Give it a clear name

Label it "Trading" or "Burner" so you never confuse it with your main wallet when a site asks you to connect.

### Step 4: Fund it from your main wallet

Send only what you plan to trade with, plus a little SOL for network fees. Keep the main wallet's address private where you can.

### Step 5: Use it for everything new

Connect the burner, never the main wallet, to new launchpads, terminals, extensions and claim pages.

## How much to keep in it

There is no right number for everyone. A useful rule is: **only what you would accept losing tonight**. Then:

- **Top up in small steps** from the main wallet when you need more.
- **Sweep profits out** to the main wallet on a schedule, not "when it feels right".
- **Keep a fee buffer** of SOL so you can always sell or move tokens.

## Habits that protect your main wallet

1. **Never enter a seed phrase or private key on a website.** No support agent, airdrop or migration needs it.
2. **Read what you sign.** If a wallet warns about an unusual transaction, stop.
3. **Type important URLs yourself.** Bookmark the sites you use.
4. **Verify contract addresses, not tickers.** Our [token checklist](/articles/how-to-check-a-solana-token-before-buying) shows how.
5. **Revoke what you no longer use.** Disconnect sites from your wallet and remove unused extensions.
6. **Keep the main wallet offline where possible.** A hardware wallet suits long-term holdings.

## Burner wallets and trading extensions

Trading extensions are where burners matter most, because the keys often live in the browser. The [Highbar Chrome extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/oioebamfkpahdnjkbbmldepkackabhed), "Highbar – Trade Solana from X", lets you trade and launch Solana tokens from posts on X. Per its store listing and Highbar's docs:

- You can **create or import several wallets**, stored locally on your device.
- Wallet keys are **created and stored only on your device**.
- Fees are shown before every trade. Extension trades add **0.02%** of the SOL amount on top of the pool fee and network fees.

That local storage is convenient, and it is exactly why you should **create a fresh burner inside the extension** rather than importing your main wallet. If anyone ever gets into your browser profile, they reach only the burner. Our guide to [trading Solana from X safely](/articles/trade-solana-from-x-chrome-extension) covers permissions and other checks.

## Burner wallets for creators

If you launch tokens, consider a dedicated launch wallet too. On Highbar, the wallet you launch with is the one that:

- signs the launch transaction, including any dev buy of up to 20 SOL,
- sees the Dev Lock button on the token page,
- claims creator fees, after signing a short free message to prove ownership.

Per the [Highbar docs](/docs), creator fees build up in the pool and are claimed by that wallet, and LP fees from the locked DAMM v2 position follow the position NFT. Keep the launch wallet's recovery phrase as carefully as your main one, since it controls those claims. You can start from the [launch page](/launch).

## What a burner wallet does not do

- It does not make any token safe.
- It does not protect you from price falls, fading hype or thin liquidity.
- It does not help if you send the main wallet's funds into it and leave them there.
- It does not replace backups.

## A one-minute checklist

- Burner has its own recovery phrase, backed up offline.
- Burner holds only what you can lose.
- Main wallet never connects to new sites.
- Extensions use a burner created inside them, not an imported main wallet.
- Profits are swept out on a schedule.

## Summary

A burner wallet is a small, separate wallet for the risky part of memecoin trading. Give it its own recovery phrase, fund it modestly, use it for every new site and extension, and sweep profits back to a main wallet that stays offline. It will not make a bad trade good, but it will keep one mistake from costing you everything.
